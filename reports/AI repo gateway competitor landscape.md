# Nobody yet grades trending AI repos by skill

State as of 7 November 2026. Built from nine research notes in `research_notes/AI repo gateway competitor landscape/`. Figures a note flagged as secondary, self-reported, page claim or unverified are marked that way here and never upgraded.

## In short

- Nobody runs a gateway that groups trending AI repos and agent skills by skill level. The one direct competitor named at launch, Vybe Guide, is offline (Vercel 404 on 7 November 2026; last archived page 1 February 2026).
- Across about 40 sites checked, skill-level grouping is absent everywhere; evidence that a listing was run is absent bar one small claim; security screening exists on skills.sh and in part at two small directories.
- Own data (n = 100, labels unreviewed): stars do not predict run-readiness (Spearman 0.08); a README-based labeller assigns a level with high or medium confidence in 97 of 100 cases, but accuracy is unmeasured.
- Recommended niche: a run-tested, screened, level-graded guide to the coding-agent stack (coding agents plus the skills and MCP servers that plug into them).
- Decision needed: approve phase 1, which is to review the 100 labels into a gold set, build the six-hourly ingestion pipeline, and sandbox-test a first batch of about 30 repos in the niche.

## Summary

No platform today combines the five things the project set out to test: grouping by skill level, grouping by AI topic, editorial curation, evidence that a listing runs, and security screening. Topic grouping and metrics-led ranking are everywhere; skill-level grouping is nowhere, and "tested" or "verified" labels, where they appear, mean paid placement or publisher identity rather than a run. GitHub, Hugging Face, the MCP and skills registries, the developer feeds and the AI-tool directories all rank by stars, likes, installs or votes. The closest thing to a competitor, Vybe Guide, launched on 30 January 2026 as a 50,760-repo star index and is dead; GitTrend claims a "difficulty estimate" that does not exist on its repo pages; AgenticSkills.io is a hand-picked list of about 195 skills whose own methodology page declines to say listed skills work. The strongest external evidence for the gap is on the trust side: a 2026 study of about 6 million suspected fake stars finds AI/LLM repos the largest category among fake-starred trending repos, and 2026 skill-registry incidents (ClawHavoc, ToxicSkills) show real malware reaching users through "top skills". The project's own 100-repo sample agrees: stars carry almost no information about setup signals, the biggest repos are polished products while the long tail is intermediate or advanced code, and 13 of 100 high-star search results had not been pushed in 180 days. The recommended first niche is the coding-agent stack for Claude Code, Cursor and Codex users, where growth is highest, beginners are over-represented at the top of the charts, and the malware pain is best documented.

## The one named competitor is dead and the near ones do not grade

**Answer: Vybe Guide is offline; GitTrend, AgenticSkills.io and claudemarket.ai have topic grouping but no level labels, no run tests and at most a publisher-identity trust score.**

Vybe Guide was the only product found that described itself as a discovery platform for vibe coders. Its press release of 30 January 2026 claimed "over 50,760" open-source projects in 19 categories, daily updates and free accounts ([AB Newswire release via FinancialContent](https://markets.financialcontent.com/woonsocketcall/article/abnewswire-2026-1-30-vybe-guide-launches-as-the-definitive-open-source-discovery-platform-for-vibe-coders-and-ai-assisted-developers)). On 7 November 2026 every route on vybeguide.ai returned a Vercel `DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND` 404, and the Wayback Machine holds HTML captures only from 31 January to 1 February 2026 ([Wayback CDX query](https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=vybeguide.ai&matchType=domain&output=txt&fl=timestamp,statuscode,mimetype,original&collapse=urlkey&filter=mimetype:text/html)). The archived home page shows a plain star-sorted index whose top five were build-your-own-x, freeCodeCamp, awesome, public-apis and free-programming-books; each card carried only name, description, stars, language and one subcategory, with **no skill level, licence, last-commit date, security score or tested mark** ([1 February 2026 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20260201084055/https://vybeguide.ai/)). The claim that it ranked on "stars, forks and recency" and showed no OSV or OpenSSF data comes from one syndicated article, not from the site ([AI CERTs, secondary](https://www.aicerts.ai/news/?p=16460)). Its operator is unnamed, no Product Hunt, X or Reddit trail was found, and the exact date it went down is unknown. The lesson is that a 50,000-repo index with an AI gloss and no trust layer can vanish within months leaving no traction trail.

GitTrend (gittrend.io) is the closest in layout. Its About page claims every repo page includes a "difficulty estimate", a "50M+ repositories tracked" figure and 57 topic categories ([GitTrend about](https://gittrend.io/about)), but raw HTML of four repo pages contained no difficulty, beginner, intermediate or advanced label, and its methodology page says the site "deliberately publishes no synthesized ratings or reviews" and ranks by star velocity ([GitTrend methodology](https://gittrend.io/methodology)). The nearest substitute is free-text prose in an AI-written "Who Should Use This" paragraph such as "Basic command-line skills needed" ([sherlock repo page](https://gittrend.io/repo/sherlock-project/sherlock)). It is ad-funded (AdSense, paid video reels, tips) and states it takes no money for ranking. Because it already holds trend data and an AI guide per repo, it is the actor most able to add a level label quickly; today it has none.

AgenticSkills.io lists about 195 skills and 141 MCP servers (index "v1.7.0, 6 November 2026") and calls itself curated, but its methodology page says listing means a skill "met the criteria above and was deliberately added", not that it was read line by line or shown to work; the automated security audit covers MCP server pages only, and the skill-side check is "still being wired in" ([AgenticSkills methodology, WebFetch summary](https://agenticskills.io/methodology)). Its grades are editorial ranks S, A and B (32, 133 and 29), not a level scale. Claudemarket.ai, readable only through a 16 October 2026 archive copy, lists "23,000+" skills, MCP servers, plugins and connectors with "27,000+ monthly visitors" (page claims) and a per-skill score whose Trust component is "Published by anthropics — an official/recognized organization", so it measures who published, not whether the code is safe ([claudemarket.ai archive](http://web.archive.org/web/20261016185906/https://www.claudemarket.ai/)). cursor.directory could not be read first-hand (HTTP 429 bot checkpoint); its "80.3k+ developers" and G2's "250,000 monthly visits" are unverified ([G2 listing](https://www.g2.com/products/cursor-directory/discuss)).

Two newer offerings edge towards the trust dimension. A scan of 115 popular AI repos published on 6 November 2026 rejected 15 for consumer-subscription sign-in, default telemetry, critical dependency vulnerabilities or winding down, using nine automated checks plus a human read ([mrtechking, WebFetch summary](https://mrtechking.com/github-ai-tools-scanned-15-rejected)). On Hacker News, RankClaw reported "scanning all 14,704 skills" on ClawHub ([HN 47208642](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47208642)) and Agensi launched a curated SKILL.md marketplace from a non-technical founder ([HN 47846681](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47846681)). None of these grades by level or publishes run results.

## GitHub and Hugging Face rank by popularity and stop there

**Answer: both are strong on topic and trend, carry no skill-level layer, no run evidence and (for GitHub) no screening; GitHub has also closed the stargazer-list API since July 2026.**

GitHub Trending offers two tabs, language filters and a Today / This week / This month window, with no AI filter, no published algorithm and no official API; third-party scrapers show about 25 repos per language and window ([GitHub Trending](https://github.com/trending); [Apify scraper, vendor claim](https://apify.com/devilscrapes/github-trending-scraper)). The `ai` topic page lists **197,069 public repositories sorted by stars by default**, with Python 62,146 and TypeScript 39,674 ([GitHub topic: ai](https://github.com/topics/ai)), and GitHub's own docs say many rankings "depend on the number of stars" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/exploring-projects-on-github/saving-repositories-with-stars)). Collections are hand-picked by the community through the github/explore repository and include "Getting started with machine learning" and "Learn to Code", the only beginner-framed content; the "good first issue" label is for contributors, not users ([GitHub Collections](https://github.com/collections); [github/explore](https://github.com/github/explore)). Octoverse 2026 reports more than 225 million developers and that agents started over 40% of pull requests in September 2026, a vendor-reported floor ([GitHub Octoverse 2026](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/)). A changelog of 30 June 2026 restricts the `/stargazers` endpoint to admins and collaborators because the data was misused for spam, so star-history work must now come from GH Archive ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-30-upcoming-access-restrictions-to-public-api-endpoints-and-ui-views/)).

Hugging Face is the strongest discovery surface for models, datasets, Spaces and papers but **does not index GitHub repositories as listings**; the Hub's object types are models, datasets, Spaces, papers, collections and kernels ([Hub API docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/api)). Its trending sort uses a Hub-computed `trendingScore` whose formula and window are unpublished; a staff-linked dataset card says it reflects "likes given by Hugging Face users in the last week" (secondary, not a documented formula) ([severo/trending-repos README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/severo/trending-repos/blob/main/README.md)). Editorial layers are Spaces of the Week, "a curated weekly selection of standout Spaces chosen by the Hugging Face team" ([Hub docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-featured)), and Trending Papers "curated by AK and the research community" with GitHub links and star counts per paper ([HF Trending Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/trending)); paperswithcode.com now redirects there after its shutdown around 24 to 25 July 2025 (secondary) ([hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/news/42900)). Learn lists 12 courses with no level or prerequisite on the listing page ([HF Learn](https://huggingface.co/learn)). Security screening is real but file-level: every file under 2 GB is run through ClamAV at each commit and pickle imports are scanned, with the docs stating the scan "is not 100% foolproof" ([Malware scanning](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-malware); [Pickle scanning](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-pickle)). Spaces' Running / Paused status is the closest live "does it run" signal in the whole survey, but it applies to hosted demos, not cloned repos ([HF Spaces](https://huggingface.co/spaces)).

The other AI hubs follow the same pattern. Replicate has an intent layer ("I want to… generate images, transcribe speech") and Official models that are "always on, maintained" ([Replicate Explore](https://replicate.com/explore)); Ollama sorts by Popular or Newest only ([Ollama library](https://ollama.com/library)); Vercel's AI templates are deployable starters within one ecosystem ([Vercel AI templates](https://vercel.com/templates/ai)); Railway lists 1,548 one-click AI templates with an undefined "(Verified)" tag on one title ([Railway AI templates](https://railway.com/deploy/category/ai)); Pinokio filters local AI apps by platform and GPU and leaves code security to app authors ([Pinokio](https://pinokio.co/)). Kaggle Learn's beginner micro-courses are known only from secondary sources because its pages are JavaScript-rendered ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/7-free-kaggle-micro-courses-for-data-science-beginners)). In every case "beginner" means "no code needed to use a demo", not "easy repo to run and change".

## Registries count servers and skills but neither test nor grade them

**Answer: the official MCP registry is deliberately unopinionated and delegates scanning; aggregators rank by popularity with identity badges; only skills.sh shows third-party security audits per listing; none groups by level.**

The official MCP Registry is "in preview", stores `server.json` metadata pointing to npm, PyPI or Docker packages, verifies only namespace ownership through GitHub, DNS or HTTP challenges, and states that security scanning is delegated to package registries and downstream aggregators; it is "deliberately unopinionated" so that aggregators add curation and ratings ([MCP Registry about](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/about)). That is an explicit invitation for a curation layer. A single-author API pull counted **30,375 unique servers on 10 September 2026**, up from about 9,650 in May 2026, with 22.9% listing no source repository and a schema that has "no auth method, security-review status, uptime check, or usage count" (secondary, one author) ([DEV: MCP registry by the numbers](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc)). Aggregator counts are not comparable: Glama showed "117,029 servers" with A to F grades for licence, quality and maintenance whose method is unexplained (page claim) ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers)); PulseMCP 21,576 with new submissions paused (page claim) ([PulseMCP](https://www.pulsemcp.com/servers)); the GitHub MCP Registry 771 ([GitHub MCP](https://github.com/mcp)). Smithery's proxy, hosting, API and CLI shut down on 31 October 2026 after it joined Arcade.dev, leaving a directory and "a curated set of verified servers" ([Smithery: future](https://smithery.ai/future)). An academic study of 67,057 servers across six registries found weak vetting let adversarial or hijacked servers reach hosts and flagged 833 vulnerable servers ([arXiv 2510.16558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16558)).

On the skills side, Anthropic runs tiered marketplaces whose web catalogue showed "529 plugins" with an undefined "Anthropic verified" label (page claim) ([Claude Marketplace](https://claude.com/marketplace/plugins)); its docs state "Anthropic doesn't review third-party marketplaces" ([Anthropic's marketplaces](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/anthropic-marketplaces.md)), every plugin carries the warning that Anthropic "cannot verify that they will work as intended", and upload scanning (pass / warn / fail) exists only for Enterprise, with the caveat that "a pass doesn't mean safe in every respect" ([Plugin security](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/security.md); [Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15927065)). skills.sh ranks by installs across 20 agents, offers topic filters, and is **the only skills site read that shows per-listing third-party security results** (Gen Agent Trust Hub, Socket and Snyk), with unaudited skills marked Pending ([skills.sh](https://skills.sh); [skills.sh audits](https://skills.sh/audits)). MCP Market claims 379,621 skills with paid Premium listings and no security badges (page claim) ([MCP Market](https://mcpmarket.com/tools/skills)). The community lists are editorial but untested: awesome-claude-skills claims "1000+" skills while its itemised list has roughly 160 entries, about 78 of them Composio's own ([awesome-claude-skills](https://github.com/ComposioHQ/awesome-claude-skills)). Template galleries for Lovable (228 templates, 21 subcategories, 14 persona filters) and v0 (13 categories) group by topic and persona, never by difficulty ([Lovable templates](https://lovable.dev/templates); [v0 templates](https://v0.app/templates)).

The security record explains why screening matters most here. Invariant Labs named the Tool Poisoning Attack on 1 April 2025, showing a tool description that read `~/.ssh/id_rsa` and recommending hash-pinning of tool descriptions ([Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-security-notification-tool-poisoning-attacks)). Pillar Security's "Rules File Backdoor" of 18 March 2025 hid instructions in Cursor and Copilot rules files with invisible Unicode ([Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/new-vulnerability-in-github-copilot-and-cursor-how-hackers-can-weaponize-code-agents)). ClawHavoc on ClawHub in January 2026 involved **1,184 malicious skills across 12 compromised publisher accounts** per OWASP's case study ([OWASP Agentic Skills Top 10](https://owasp.github.io/www-project-agentic-skills-top-10/case-studies)), with Koi Security separately counting 341 malicious among 2,857 audited skills ([eSecurityPlanet](https://esecurityplanet.com/threats/hundreds-of-malicious-skills-found-in-openclaws-clawhub)). Snyk's ToxicSkills study of 5 February 2026 scanned 3,984 skills from ClawHub and skills.sh and found **36.82% with any flaw, 13.4% with a critical issue and 76 confirmed malicious payloads** ([Snyk](https://snyk.io/blog/toxicskills-malicious-ai-agent-skills-clawhub/)). Island's FakeGit research (reported 21 July 2026) counted about 7,600 malicious GitHub repos, 800+ posing as AI skills or MCP servers, and showed Claude Code, Gemini and ChatGPT each passing on install steps from malicious repos; download totals may be inflated by bots ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/21/github-repos-malware-campaign-fakegit-ai-agents/)). The ReadSecBench study found hidden README instructions led agents to exfiltrate data in up to 85% of cases and that none of 15 human reviewers spotted them ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/17/ai-agents-readme-files-data-leak-security-risk)), so a gateway must not render README text to an agent unsanitised. Among user voices, the sharpest was an HN comment on 5 February 2026: "most of the top skills are malware" ([HN 46899686](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46899686)).

## News feeds and directories sell attention, not trust

**Answer: feeds and newsletters rank by engagement and sell sponsorship; directories sell "verified" badges; only Hacker News's Show HN has a runnable rule, and only Console writes reasoned pros and cons.**

Every developer feed ranks on votes or composite traction. daily.dev claims "1 million+ active developers" (an advertising claim) and is the only feed with a documented public API, tag-filtered feeds and an MCP server, at 300 requests per minute per IP and 60 per user ([daily.dev API docs](https://docs.daily.dev/docs/plus/public-api); [daily.dev blog](https://daily.dev/blog/developer-marketing-platforms-comparison-channel-mix/)). Hacker News's Show HN rule requires "something people can run or try" and excludes "quickly generated one-offs" because "anybody can do that now" ([Show HN rules](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)); secondary blogs report Show HN submissions tripled under AI-built projects and moderators restricting new accounts in March 2026, with no official announcement found ([keydiscussions, secondary](https://keydiscussions.com/2026/03/09/hacker-news-moves-toward-restricting-show-hn-posts-amid-the-ai-slop-wave/)). Peerlist Launchpad ranks weekly on an aggregate of upvotes, comments, views and traction, with verified-profile votes weighing more ([Peerlist help](https://help.peerlist.io/individual/launchpad/guidelines-faqs)). r/opensource reportedly barred AI projects from 24 October 2026 (secondary) ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/26/reddit-r-opensource-ai-ban)). The floods on HN and Reddit are themselves the clearest demand signal for a filtered, trust-led layer.

Newsletters are competitors for attention and potential distribution partners, not ingestible sources. TLDR AI's advertiser page lists about 1.15 million subscribers with a 47% open rate (self-reported; other sources say 1 million to 1.25 million) ([TLDR advertise](https://advertise.tldr.tech/audiences/ai-engineers/)); Ben's Bites claims 140,000 to 150,000 (self-reported) ([Ben's Bites ads](https://ads.bensbites.com/)); a roundup puts The Rundown AI at "about 1 million" or "2M+" and The Batch at 1.7 million, all unverified ([Creative AI News, secondary](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/ai-newsletter-landscape-2026/)). None found rates difficulty or tested status. Console, with 30,000+ subscribers (self-reported), reviews two or three tools a week with "What we like" and "What we don't like", the nearest to reasoned editorial review, but states no test method ([Console](https://console.dev/)). Changelog Nightly lists top new and top starred GitHub projects nightly with no stated grouping ([Changelog Nightly](https://changelog.com/nightly)).

Directories are product-centred and monetise listings. There's An AI For That claims 50,000+ tools, 40,000+ repositories and 4.5 million monthly visits, with "Staff review every submission before it goes live" (self-reported) ([TAAFT about](https://theresanaiforthat.com/about/)); it is the only directory found indexing repositories, and the material read shows no level grouping or run evidence. Futurepedia sells a "Verified Enhanced Listing + 1,000 Clicks" for $497 ([Futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io/enhanced)) and Toolify's "verified" badge is tied to paid placement per vendor sources ([poweredbyai.app, vendor](https://poweredbyai.app/alternatives/toolify)), so **"verified" in directories means paid, not tested**. On the economics, cursor.directory reportedly earns about $34,000 to $35,000 a month from job listings (Starter Story, self-reported) ([Starter Story summary](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/my-website-makes-35k-month-built-in-3-hours?t=0)), a small Flippa listing shows the low end at $1,500 to $2,600 a month ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/13392265-profitable-ai-tools-directory-generating-1-5k-2-6k-month-with-dr-71-3k-organic-visitors-multiple-revenue-streams-and-huge-growth-potential)), and Trendshift sells a $9 per month Signal plan for AI search and API ([Trendshift Signal](https://trendshift.io/signal)). Money flows to those with an owned audience, so the gateway's distribution (a digest) matters as much as its index. Freshness is a live risk: one directory's own count has 395 of 2,862 tracked AI tools retired (13.8%), with retirements rising from 23 in 2023 to 156 in 2026 (self-published, unstable between snapshots) ([ToolDirectory.AI](https://tooldirectory.ai/research/ai-tool-mortality)).

## Free APIs cover a six-hourly refresh; Reddit and GitHub Trending do not

**Answer: ingest from the GitHub REST and GraphQL APIs, GH Archive, deps.dev and Scorecard, the Hugging Face API, the HN Firebase API, npm and PyPI download APIs, and registry APIs; avoid scraping GitHub Trending, treat Product Hunt as permission-only and write Reddit off after March 2027.**

The GitHub REST API allows 5,000 requests per hour with a token (60 unauthenticated), so one token gives up to 30,000 calls per six-hour window ([GitHub rate limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)); GraphQL gives 5,000 points per hour, with aliased queries for 100 repos costing a few points, enough for several thousand repos per refresh with release, commit and contributor fields ([GitHub GraphQL limits](https://docs.github.com/en/graphql/overview/rate-limits-and-node-limits-for-the-graphql-api)). Star velocity and spike detection must be rebuilt on GH Archive's hourly JSON files or its BigQuery dataset (1 TB free a month), since the stargazer-list endpoint is closed ([gharchive.org](https://www.gharchive.org/)); a six-hour slice is six hourly files, which a free GitHub Action can stream. OpenSSF Scorecard scans more than 1.3 million repos weekly across 18 checks, including Maintained, Pinned-Dependencies, CI-Tests and Dangerous-Workflow, and serves results through a REST API; deps.dev mirrors it free with stars, forks, licence and OSS-Fuzz data, no key or rate limit stated ([ossf/scorecard](https://github.com/ossf/scorecard); [deps.dev API](https://docs.deps.dev/api/v3/)). Most small AI repos will not be in the weekly scan, so show "not scanned" rather than zero. Hugging Face allows 500 anonymous or 1,000 free-user API requests per five minutes and exposes `trendingScore` as a sort field ([HF rate limits](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/rate-limits); [OpenAPI spec](https://huggingface.co/.well-known/openapi.md)). npm download counts take bulk requests of up to 128 packages ([npm download-counts](https://github.com/npm/registry/blob/main/docs/download-counts.md)) and pypistats gives 180 days of history ([pypistats API](https://pypistats.org/api/)).

For mentions, the Hacker News Firebase API is MIT-licensed with "currently no rate limit" ([HN API](https://github.com/HackerNews/API)), but Y Combinator's site terms prohibit scraping and commercial republishing of site content (as summarised), so keep to titles and links ([YC legal](https://www.ycombinator.com/legal/)). daily.dev's API permits personal and own-token integrations and reserves competing uses to a Terms section not retrieved ([daily.dev ToS](https://daily.dev/tos/)). Product Hunt's API "must not be used for commercial purposes" by default ([Product Hunt API docs](https://api.producthunt.com/v2/docs)). Reddit ends RSS on 13 November 2026 and closes public API access to remaining apps in March 2027 (secondary reports; Reddit domains could not be fetched) ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/social-media/reddit-rss-feeds-ending-public-api-shutdown-2027)). GitHub Trending has no API and scraping its HTML is a separate legal question the sources do not settle, so compute velocity from the API instead ([Apify scraper, vendor](https://apify.com/themineworks/github-trending-scraper)). The official MCP Registry API returns paged metadata without totals ([Registry API](https://registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0.1/servers?limit=1)), and skills.sh, Glama and PulseMCP expose rankings on their pages. None of this is legal advice; a lawyer should check the terms before launch.

## Coding agents, skills and local runners lead the topic field

**Answer: growth and beginner demand both point at the coding-agent stack; inference serving, fine-tuning and evals are hot but advanced.**

GitHub publishes ranked lists rather than per-category growth, so the ranking below is a synthesis. Octoverse 2025 counted more than 4.3 million AI-related repositories and 1.13 million public repos importing an LLM SDK, up 178% year on year, with six of the ten fastest-growing repos by contributors being AI infrastructure (cline, vllm, ragflow, sglang, continue, ComfyUI) and MCP reaching 37,000 stars in eight months ([GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)). Octoverse 2026's fastest-growing repos by contributors were cc-switch, awesome-mcp-servers, omarchy, first-contributions, llama.cpp, sglang and vllm among community projects, and claude-code, codex, opencode, copilot-cli, gemini-cli, litellm and kilocode among vendor projects ([GitHub Octoverse 2026](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/)). Star History's 200,000-star table shows **OpenClaw at 388,577 stars on 2 September 2026 after passing 200,000 in 88 days**, Hermes Agent at 239,699, OpenCode at 203,145 and n8n at 203,097; its claim that DeepSeek Harness reached 200,000 in 15 days is single-source ([Star History blog](https://www.star-history.com/blog/deepseek-harness-beats-openclaw-fastest-200k-stars)). Several 2026 top gainers (Everything Claude Code, awesome-design-md) are config and skill packs rather than software, which is why stars measure attention, not use.

Beginner demand is best measured by Stack Overflow 2025: 84% of respondents use or plan to use AI tools, 39.5% of learners use AI daily, Ollama is the most-used agent orchestration tool at 51.1% (LangChain 32.9%), RAG is used by 10.6%, and 72.2% say vibe coding is not part of their professional work ([Stack Overflow 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai); [technology section](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)). Those respondents are developers, so true-beginner demand is under-measured. Microsoft's three "for Beginners" repos hold about 270,000 stars combined yet grade by lesson type, not level ([generative-ai-for-beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners); [ai-agents-for-beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/ai-agents-for-beginners); [AI-For-Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/AI-For-Beginners)); datawhalechina/easy-vibe (about 20,400 stars) is the one repo found that grades vibe-coding material in three explicit stages, the advanced stage covering Claude Code, MCP, skills and RAG ([easy-vibe](https://github.com/datawhalechina/easy-vibe)). Lovable reports about 80% of its builders self-identify as non-technical (vendor data) ([Lovable blog](https://lovable.dev/en/blog/a-first-look-at-the-build-economy)) and Vercel says "About 63% of users exploring vibe tools are non-developers" (unsourced on the page) ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-vibe-coding)), but those users mostly stay in browser builders; the addressable group for a repo gateway is novices and tinkerers using Claude Code, Cursor and Codex locally. US searches for "vibe coding" ran at about 110,000 a month in mid-2026, down from a March 2026 peak near 165,000 (a commercial directory's estimate) ([vibecoding.app](https://vibecoding.app/blog/state-of-vibe-coding-2026)), and MCP search interest peaked around August 2025 even as server counts kept rising ([Glama, State of MCP 2025](https://glama.ai/blog/2025-12-07-the-state-of-mcp-in-2025)), which suggests skills and config packs are the 2026 successor to MCP as the beginner's entry point.

The project's own sample confirms the fit between topic and level (own computation, labels unreviewed): coding agents were labelled beginner in six of seven cases and MCP servers or skills in nine of fourteen, while fine-tuning was advanced in six of six and voice in four of six; RAG and browser automation were mostly intermediate.

## Stars fail as a quality signal and the project's own data agrees

**Answer: published research finds stars track promotion rather than upkeep and shows fake-star campaigns concentrated in AI repos; the project's 100-repo pull finds Spearman 0.08 between stars and setup signals, and a README labeller that is cheap but unvalidated.**

The ICSE 2026 StarScout study counted about 6.0 million suspected fake stars before post-processing and **3.81 million across 18,617 repositories after it**; fake stars were at most about 1% of monthly stars yet in July 2024 16.66% of popular repos had campaigns, AI/LLM repos were 16.6% of the categorised set and the largest category among fake-starred trending repos (25 of 78, 32.1%), and GitHub had deleted 90.42% of flagged repos but only 57.07% of accounts by January 2025 ([arXiv 2412.13459 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2)). A 1% rise in fake stars bought about 0.07% more real stars the next month and a slightly negative long-run effect, so fake stars help only briefly ([same](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2)). Socket's "Suspicious Stars" alert exists because of that work ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/3-7-million-fake-github-stars-a-growing-threat-linked-to-scams-and-malware)). Older work finds developers star to show appreciation or bookmark, and that growth follows promotion bursts ([Borges and Valente, arXiv 1811.07643, from a search summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07643v1)); a model flagging unmaintained projects from commit, fork and issue activity reached 80% precision and 96% recall without using stars ([Coelho et al., arXiv 1809.04041](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04041)). The literature is mixed, since a 2023 study treats stars as a reflective quality measure (search snippet only) ([arXiv 2307.12082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12082)). No study measures how often trending repos fail to run; the nearest evidence is that only 24.11% of 863,878 GitHub notebook executions ran without error ([NSF PAR 10311570, via search summary](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10311570)) and 35.4% of 12,720 popular Kaggle notebooks remain reproducible ([arXiv 2602.07195](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07195v1)). No published rubric scores a repository's ease to run; the ACM artifact badges (Available, Functional, Reusable) are the nearest established scheme and do not rate difficulty ([PLDI 2026 artifacts call, via search summary](https://pldi26.sigplan.org/track/pldi-2026-pldi-research-artifacts)).

The project's own test (own computation from a GitHub search API pull of 7 November 2026, 100 repos across 17 AI topic queries, stars 360 to 439,103, median 28,917, reproducible from `data/labelled_repos_for_review.csv`) reaches the same conclusion. **Spearman rho between stars and a seven-signal readiness score (Dockerfile, devcontainer, deploy or Colab badge, quickstart heading, lockfile, .github folder, licence file) was 0.081.** Per-signal correlations ranged from -0.29 (deploy badge, contaminated by Colab and Spaces links on research repos) to 0.18 (quickstart). Mean readiness by star band was 3.21 of 7 under 5,000 stars, 3.90 for 5,000 to 20,000, 4.43 for 20,000 to 60,000 and 3.58 above 60,000, so readiness peaks in the middle and falls for the most-starred repos. Thirteen of 100 had not been pushed in 180 days (for example AUTOMATIC1111/stable-diffusion-webui, coqui-ai/TTS, haotian-liu/LLaVA, SciPhi-AI/R2R), with no warning in search results. No repo was executed, so "works first try" remains untested and the score is a proxy.

On the second assumption, a README-only Claude Sonnet labeller assigned candidate levels of beginner 31, intermediate 33, advanced 28 and not_runnable 8, with confidence high in 43, medium in 54 and low in 3. Beginner-labelled repos had the highest median stars (103,218) against intermediate 28,365 and advanced 14,003; the top 50 by stars held 23 beginner labels and the bottom 50 held 8. The reading is that the head of a star chart is polished products while the long tail is intermediate or advanced code, so **a level filter changes the middle and tail of a list, not the head**. These labels are candidates awaiting the owner's review, not a gold set; the brief's target is 85% agreement with the reviewed set, and the mechanical signals alone would not have graded the repos, the README reading did most of the work. External gateways offer no help on criteria: roadmap.sh's project filters are beginner / intermediate / advanced with no stated definition ([roadmap.sh projects](https://roadmap.sh/ai-engineer/projects)), DeepLearning.AI tags 65 Beginner and 69 Intermediate short courses without defining either ([DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/)), and open-source onboarding tools use a single "good first issue" signal ([goodfirstissue.dev](https://goodfirstissue.dev/)). A level defined by measurable run requirements (API key, Docker, GPU, cloud account) would therefore be new.

## The unclaimed combination is level plus run evidence plus screening

**Answer: nobody combines a level label, a recorded run test and a security screen over a topic-grouped index; the pieces exist separately and the official MCP registry explicitly leaves curation to others.**

Each dimension has a partial owner. Topic grouping belongs to Trendshift (about 64 AI-categorised topics) ([Trendshift topics](https://trendshift.io/topics)), WhatsTrending.ai (MCP, RAG, coding agents, agent frameworks, with a stated "does not audit or verify") ([WhatsTrending.ai](https://whatstrending.ai/repos)) and the Glama and skills.sh category filters. Editorial curation belongs to awesome lists, which gatekeep admission but go stale (Awesome-LLM last pushed 31 July 2025) or become vendor-run (e2b-dev/awesome-ai-agents with 1,100 open pull requests) ([Awesome-LLM](https://github.com/Hannibal046/Awesome-LLM); [awesome-ai-agents](https://github.com/e2b-dev/awesome-ai-agents)). Liveness checks exist: glincker/awesome-selfhosted-ai is "health-checked weekly" by reading GitHub metadata, not by running anything ([DEV post](https://dev.to/thegdsks/i-built-a-self-hosted-ai-list-that-checks-itself-weekly-3m0a)). Security screening exists on skills.sh and in Hugging Face's file scans. Nobody clones, installs and launches a repo in a clean container and publishes the result with time and failures; nobody labels a level by run requirements; and nobody pairs those with a star-spike and clone check of the kind FakeGit makes necessary.

Three things are therefore worth doing and undone. First, an honest run test ("setup signals present" until a sandbox build has passed, then "installed in N minutes in a clean container on date X"), loosely modelled on ACM Functional. Second, a level scale defined by what the user must have (nothing, Python, Docker, GPU, paid API key, cloud account), which also gives beginners a reason to trust the label. Third, a provenance and screening layer that combines Scorecard's Dangerous-Workflow and Token-Permissions checks, deps.dev vulnerability data, a star-to-age and lockstep check from GH Archive, and a typosquat check against known names, shown per listing with the method published, since "verified" badges elsewhere are paid or identity-only. Demand evidence for the beginner setup pain is indirect (Fiverr gigs selling paid fixes for broken OpenClaw and Hermes installs ([example gig](https://fiverr.com/james_trainor/setup-fix-install-openclaw-hermes-agent-open-claw-developer-mac-mini-vps-docker)); a May 2026 preprint finding less-experienced vibe coders rely on re-prompting and report confusion when code breaks ([arXiv 2605.24521](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24521))), while demand evidence for the trust pain is strong and documented. The design should lead with trust and runnability and treat the level label as the organising view, not the sole promise.

### Recommended niche

Start with **the coding-agent stack for Claude Code, Cursor and Codex users: coding agents and harnesses, plus the skills, rules packs and MCP servers that plug into them**, graded by run requirements, sandbox-tested and screened. It is the fastest-growing area in both Octoverse reports, beginner-heavy at the top of the charts in the project's own sample (six of seven coding agents and nine of fourteen skills or MCP servers labelled beginner, unreviewed), the area with the best-documented malware pain (ClawHavoc, ToxicSkills, FakeGit), and the one where the official registry says in writing that curation is for others to add. Begin with about 30 to 50 items and a weekly digest, since newsletters show that distribution, not index size, earns revenue.

### Ranked list of ten topics

1. Coding agents and harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Cline, Gemini CLI): highest growth, highest learner demand, mostly beginner in own data.
2. Agent skills, rules and config packs: the biggest 2026 star gainers; the strongest case for screening.
3. MCP servers: largest registry volume with no grading or testing anywhere; search interest cooling, so quality signals are the gap.
4. Local LLM runners (Ollama, llama.cpp, Open WebUI): most-used orchestration tool in Stack Overflow 2025; beginner-friendly.
5. Personal and autonomous agents (OpenClaw, Hermes, OpenManus): the largest star phenomenon; paid install-fix gigs show setup pain.
6. Workflow and agent builders (n8n, Dify, RAGFlow): large and low-code.
7. RAG and memory tools: 10.6% use in Stack Overflow 2025; mostly intermediate in own data.
8. Browser automation and computer use (browser-use, Playwright MCP): mostly intermediate in own data.
9. AI app builders and vibe-coding starters (bolt.diy, AI templates): strong learner search interest; galleries exist but ungraded.
10. Image and video generation tooling (ComfyUI): beginner demand likely, growth evidence thin, staleness risk (AUTOMATIC1111 idle over 180 days).

Deferred: inference serving (vLLM, SGLang), LLM gateways, evals and observability, and fine-tuning, all fast-growing but advanced (fine-tuning was advanced in six of six own-data cases).

### Assumption-test results (own computation, labels unreviewed)

- **Do stars predict run-readiness? No.** Spearman rho 0.081 between stars and the seven-signal score over 100 repos; readiness peaks at 20,000 to 60,000 stars (4.43 of 7) and falls above 60,000 (3.58); 13 of 100 high-star results were idle over 180 days. Caveats: n = 100 drawn by stars, signals are proxies, no repo was executed, the deploy-badge signal is contaminated. Published work on fake stars and unmaintained projects points the same way.
- **Can a skill level be assigned reliably? Cheap to produce, not yet shown to be right.** The README-only labeller gave a level with high or medium confidence in 97 of 100 cases and the levels align with topic in plausible ways, but the labels are unreviewed candidates with no measured agreement; the 85% agreement target against an owner-reviewed gold set is the phase 1 test. Levels elsewhere are self-declared without criteria, so a run-requirement definition is both new and testable.
